\section{Milestone M2: error mitigation}
\label{sec:m2}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=gray!5, colframe=gray!40, title={M2 at a glance}, fonttitle=\bfseries]

\subsection{Goal and exit criteria}

After M0 the noisy energy is an honest estimate, but it sits \VMtHvaZeroRawRel{} above the truth at
the exact parameters (\cref{sec:noise}). An optimiser cannot remove that bias
(\cref{prop:mixed}); mitigation can try. M2 implemented four techniques as composable estimator
layers, measured each combination at \emph{fixed} parameters where the noise-free energy is
known, and chose a stack for full optimisations and later hardware runs. Exit: a measured bias
and shot cost for every stack, and a chosen stack. The milestone closed twice. The first choice
was withdrawn after this report found the cancellation of \cref{sec:m2cancel}; the second was
validated against one error source at a time (\cref{sec:m2sources}).

\subsection{Theory}

All four techniques act on the energy estimator, not on the circuit's parameters. Two of them
need the individual shots, so they replace the Estimator primitive with a sampler that measures
the \VMeasGroups{} bases itself. Since every term of $H$ is $X_iX_j$, $Y_iY_j$ or $Z_iZ_j$, the
three qubit-wise commuting groups are the uniform bases: all qubits measured in $X$, all in $Y$,
or all in $Z$.

\begin{ntn}
  For one basis, a shot is a bit string $b\in\{0,1\}^{n}$, $n=\VNumSites$, and the noise-free
  outcome distribution is $p(b)$. Qubit $q$'s readout flips $0\to1$ with probability $e_0^{(q)}$
  and $1\to0$ with probability $e_1^{(q)}$, independently of the other qubits; its confusion
  matrix is
  $A_q=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1-e_0^{(q)} & e_1^{(q)}\\ e_0^{(q)} & 1-e_1^{(q)}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
  with columns indexed by the prepared bit and rows by the read bit. A Pauli term $P_t$ of the
  measured basis acts on the support $S_t$, and $s(b)=(-1)^{b}$ is the eigenvalue of bit $b$.
\end{ntn}

\begin{dfn}[Counts-level energy estimator]\label{dfn:counts}
  Given weights $w_q\colon\{0,1\}\to\RR$, a shot $b$ contributes
  $\varepsilon(b)=\sum_t c_t\prod_{q\in S_t}w_q(b_q)$ and the energy estimate is the mean of
  $\varepsilon$ over the shots, summed over the three bases. Without readout correction
  $w_q=s$. With readout correction $w_q=v_q$, where $v_q=(1,-1)\,A_q^{-1}$, that is
  \[
    v_q(0)=\frac{1-e_1^{(q)}+e_0^{(q)}}{1-e_0^{(q)}-e_1^{(q)}},\qquad
    v_q(1)=-\frac{1+e_1^{(q)}-e_0^{(q)}}{1-e_0^{(q)}-e_1^{(q)}} .
  \]
  \emph{Symmetry filtering} discards every shot whose Hamming weight $|b|$ differs from $n/2$
  before the mean is taken.
\end{dfn}

Because $\varepsilon$ is computed per shot, the standard error of the mean is the exact one,
$\operatorname{std}(\varepsilon)/\sqrt{\text{shots}}$, instead of the primitive's upper bound
of \cref{rem:stdbound}. (The estimator of \cref{dfn:ctf} below is not a per-shot mean; its
error comes from a bootstrap.)

\begin{prop}[Readout correction is unbiased]\label{prop:readout}
  If the readout errors are independent with confusion matrices $A_q$ and the $v_q$ use the same
  $A_q$, then for every Pauli term $\mathbb E\bigl[\prod_{q\in S_t}v_q(\tilde b_q)\bigr]=\langle P_t\rangle$,
  where $\tilde b$ is the read string and $\langle P_t\rangle$ the noise-free expectation.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
  Condition on the prepared string $b$. The read bits are independent with
  $\Pr(\tilde b_q=r\mid b_q)=(A_q)_{r b_q}$, so
  $\mathbb E[v_q(\tilde b_q)\mid b_q]=\sum_r v_q(r)(A_q)_{r b_q}=\bigl((1,-1)A_q^{-1}A_q\bigr)_{b_q}=s(b_q)$.
  Independence turns the expectation of the product over $S_t$ into the product of these
  conditional means, $\prod_{q\in S_t}s(b_q)$, which is the eigenvalue of $P_t$ on $b$. Averaging
  over $b\sim p$ gives $\langle P_t\rangle$.
\end{proof}

The price is variance: $|v_q|\approx1/(1-2e)$, so each two-body term's per-shot magnitude grows
by about $1/(1-2e)^2$. With the calibrated mean flip probability $e=\VMtReadoutErr$ (largest
\VMtReadoutErrMax) this is under $10\,\%$.

\begin{prop}[Ring-ansatz states lie in the zero-magnetisation sector of every basis]\label{prop:sector}
  For every parameter value, the RVB and HVA states $\ket{\psi(\theta)}$ of \cref{dfn:ansatz}
  are total-spin singlets, $\vec S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\,2}\ket{\psi(\theta)}=0$ with
  $\vec S_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_i\vec\sigma_i/2$. Consequently, in each uniform basis $X$, $Y$ or
  $Z$, a noise-free shot has exactly $n/2$ ones.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
  The initial state is a product of singlets (\cref{dfn:singlet}), each with total spin $0$, so
  the product has $\vec S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\,2}=0$. Every later gate is generated by
  $\SWAP_{ij}=\tfrac12(I+X_iX_j+Y_iY_j+Z_iZ_j)$ or by $h_{ij}$, both functions of
  $\vec\sigma_i\cdot\vec\sigma_j$, which commutes with every component of $\vec S_{\mathrm{tot}}$.
  Hence each gate commutes with $\vec S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\,2}$ and the state stays a singlet.
  Now $0=\bra\psi\vec S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\,2}\ket\psi=\sum_{\alpha}\lVert S^\alpha_{\mathrm{tot}}\ket\psi\rVert^2$,
  so $S^\alpha_{\mathrm{tot}}\ket\psi=0$ for $\alpha=x,y,z$. Measuring every qubit in basis
  $\alpha$ measures the $\sigma^\alpha_i$, and $S^\alpha_{\mathrm{tot}}=\tfrac12(n-2|b|)$ on outcome
  $b$; only $|b|=n/2$ has nonzero amplitude.
\end{proof}

A shot of any other weight was corrupted by noise and can be discarded \parencite{bonetmonroig2018,mcardle2019}.
This is not free: the filter keeps only about \VMtKept{} of the shots under full device noise,
so the shot noise per energy grows by about $\sqrt2$. It also only detects errors that change
the weight; an error that maps the state to another weight-$n/2$ string passes.

Combining the two layers needs care, because filtering changes the distribution that the
calibration describes (\cref{sec:m2cancel}). The consistent order is the reverse.

\begin{dfn}[Correct, then filter]\label{dfn:ctf}
  Let $\tilde p$ be the read distribution of one basis and $A=\bigotimes_q A_q$ the calibrated
  readout channel. Form the quasi-probability vector $\hat p=A^{-1}\tilde p$, set every entry with
  $|b|\ne n/2$ to zero, divide by the remaining total weight $\sum_{|b|=n/2}\hat p(b)$ (the
  \emph{kept quasi-weight}), and evaluate $\sum_t c_t\,\langle P_t\rangle$ on the result with
  $w_q=s$.
\end{dfn}

\begin{prop}[Correct, then filter removes readout error exactly]\label{prop:ctf}
  Let $q$ be the outcome distribution of one basis \emph{before} readout, so that
  $\tilde p=Aq$, and let the calibration be exact. Then the estimator of \cref{dfn:ctf} equals
  the symmetry-filtered energy of $q$. In particular, if the only noise is readout error, it
  returns the noise-free energy.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
  $A$ is invertible when every $e_0^{(q)}+e_1^{(q)}<1$, and $A^{-1}\tilde p=A^{-1}Aq=q$. The
  remaining steps of \cref{dfn:ctf} are exactly symmetry filtering (\cref{dfn:counts}) applied
  to $q$. If readout is the only noise, $q=p$, which is supported on $|b|=n/2$ by
  \cref{prop:sector}; filtering leaves it unchanged, the kept weight is $1$, and the energy is
  the noise-free one.
\end{proof}

So under full noise this estimator sees only the gate noise, minus whatever the filter removes:
the exact computation of \cref{sec:m2cancel} gives \VMxHvaZeroFullCtf{} for HVA at $\theta=0$
with full noise and \VMxHvaZeroGtCtf{} with gate noise alone, equal to machine precision. With a finite calibration and finite shots,
$\hat p$ has negative entries and the kept weight fluctuates, so the variance is larger than
for filtering alone. The inversion acts on all $2^n$ outcomes, which limits it to about 20
qubits.

\begin{dfn}[Zero-noise extrapolation by local folding]\label{dfn:zne}
  For an odd scale $\lambda=2k+1$, replace every two-qubit gate $G$ of the transpiled circuit by
  $G(G^\dagger G)^k$ \parencite{giurgicatiron2020}. The unitary is unchanged and the two-qubit
  error is amplified roughly $\lambda$-fold. Measure $E(\lambda)$ at $\lambda=1,3,5$ and fit
  $E(\lambda)=A\,e^{-b\lambda}$ \parencite{endo2018}; report $A=E(0)$.
\end{dfn}

The exponential model is the one suggested by \cref{prop:depol}: if each folded gate multiplied
the energy by a fixed factor, $E(\lambda)$ would decay geometrically in $\lambda$. ZNE triples
the shots per energy.

\begin{dfn}[Twin-circuit rescaling]\label{dfn:twin}
  Choose \emph{twin} parameters $\theta_{\mathrm{t}}$ whose noise-free energy
  $E_{\mathrm{ideal}}(\theta_{\mathrm{t}})$ is computable classically, measure the twin once with
  noise, and report $E_{\mathrm{mit}}(\theta)=E_{\mathrm{noisy}}(\theta)\,
  E_{\mathrm{ideal}}(\theta_{\mathrm{t}})/E_{\mathrm{noisy}}(\theta_{\mathrm{t}})$
  \parencite{urbanek2021}. The package picks the twin on a grid of multiples of $\pi/4$ with the
  largest $|E_{\mathrm{ideal}}|$, excluding ground states so the test is not circular.
\end{dfn}

\begin{rem}[When rescaling is exact]
  Under global depolarising noise of the same strength for both circuits, \cref{prop:depol} gives
  $E_{\mathrm{noisy}}=(1-p)E_{\mathrm{ideal}}$ for target and twin alike, and the ratio cancels
  $(1-p)$ exactly. Rescaling therefore works to the extent that the damping factor
  $E_{\mathrm{noisy}}/E_{\mathrm{ideal}}$ is the same for the twin and the target. Nothing
  guarantees that for real noise, which is neither global nor state-independent.
\end{rem}

\begin{prop}[Rescaling error]\label{prop:twinerr}
  Write $D=E_{\mathrm{noisy}}(\theta)/E_{\mathrm{ideal}}(\theta)$ and
  $D_{\mathrm t}=E_{\mathrm{noisy}}(\theta_{\mathrm t})/E_{\mathrm{ideal}}(\theta_{\mathrm t})$
  for the damping of target and twin, measured with infinitely many shots. Then twin rescaling
  has relative bias
  $\bigl(E_{\mathrm{mit}}-E_{\mathrm{ideal}}\bigr)/|E_{\mathrm{ideal}}|=1-D/D_{\mathrm t}$
  when $E_{\mathrm{ideal}}(\theta)<0$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
  By \cref{dfn:twin},
  $E_{\mathrm{mit}}=E_{\mathrm{noisy}}(\theta)\,E_{\mathrm{ideal}}(\theta_{\mathrm t})/E_{\mathrm{noisy}}(\theta_{\mathrm t})
  =D\,E_{\mathrm{ideal}}(\theta)/D_{\mathrm t}$. Subtract $E_{\mathrm{ideal}}(\theta)$ and divide
  by $|E_{\mathrm{ideal}}(\theta)|=-E_{\mathrm{ideal}}(\theta)$.
\end{proof}

A twin damped \emph{more} than the target ($D_{\mathrm t}<D$) makes the estimate overshoot
below the noise-free value; a twin damped less leaves part of the bias.

\subsection{Implementation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Counts layer} (\py{CountsEnergy} in \py{mitigation/counts.py}): measures the three
    bases itself, with Qiskit's backend sampler over Aer (fresh seed per call, as in M0) or the runtime
    \py{SamplerV2}. Readout is calibrated once per estimator by preparing all-zeros and all-ones
    (2 circuits of 16384 shots). In \texttt{c9272e3}, which produced the first study, shots were
    \emph{filtered first} and then weighted with $v_q$. Since \texttt{56aadd3} the combination
    follows \cref{dfn:ctf}, with a bootstrap standard error from 50 resamples of the shots.
  \item \textbf{Noise switch} (\texttt{56aadd3}): \py{backend.noise} selects \py{full},
    \py{readout} or \py{gates}, so the same configuration can be evaluated with one error source
    at a time.
  \item \textbf{ZNE} (\py{ZneEnergy}): folds the ISA circuit with
    \py{transpile.fold_two_qubit_gates} once per scale and wraps one inner estimator per scale, so
    readout calibration is repeated per scale. Only the single-qubit measurement suffix is
    translated after folding; re-optimising would cancel the inserted $G^\dagger G$ pairs.
  \item \textbf{Twin} (\py{TwinRescaledEnergy}, \py{choose_twin}): measures the twin once at
    \VMbTwinShots{} shots per basis when the estimator opens, through the same inner stack, and
    propagates its uncertainty into the reported standard error. \py{twin = "auto"} searches the
    $\pi/4$ grid with a statevector, which also limits it to about 20 qubits.
  \item \textbf{Composition}: \py{make_estimator} builds counts processing innermost, then ZNE,
    then rescaling, from a \py{[mitigation]} table in the run config.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation mode}: \py{kagome-vqe benchmark CONFIG --point P --repeats R}
    estimates the energy $R$ times at all parameters equal to $P$, with fresh seeds, and writes
    \py{evaluation.json} with the mean, the real spread, the bias against the noise-free energy,
    the shot cost, the kept fraction and a \py{below_ground} flag for means more than two
    standard errors below $E_0$. Until \texttt{2116da4} all $R$ repeats shared one estimator,
    hence one calibration and one twin measurement (the \py{m2b} reruns); since then each repeat
    builds its own (the \py{m2c} reruns reported below).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why HVA's twin fails without the filter.} HVA's twin is at $(5\pi/4,5\pi/4)$ with
noise-free energy \VMtTwinIdealHva{} and noisy energy \VMtTwinNoisyHva, a rescaling factor
\VMtTwinFactorHva. The twin is damped more than the target, so by \cref{prop:twinerr} the
factor over-corrects (\cref{sec:m2twin} gives the damping factors). RVB's twin (noise-free \VMtTwinIdealRvb, noisy \VMtTwinNoisyRvb, factor
\VMtTwinFactorRvb) happens to be damped like the target, and rescaling works. The development
session also notes that on the Clifford grid ($\pi/2$ steps) every HVA point is a ground state
or has zero energy, so no Clifford twin exists for HVA on this lattice.

\paragraph{Why symmetry and ZNE do not stack.} The development session's explanation:
post-selection removes noise in a way that is not multiplicative in $\lambda$ (the kept fraction
itself falls with $\lambda$), so the exponential fit extrapolates a curve that does not have the
assumed shape. The observed overshoot, \VMtHvaZeroRoSymZneRel{} at $\theta=0$, is consistent with
that, and the reruns show it with gate noise alone (\VMbHvaGatesPtRoSymZneRel), but this report
has not checked the mechanism separately.

\subsection{Why readout plus symmetry looks accurate}
\label{sec:m2cancel}

The development session attributed the small overshoot of readout + symmetry to the calibration
describing all shots while being applied to the post-selected ones. To test that, and to measure
how large the effect is, this report computed the \emph{infinite-shot} value of every
counts-level estimator exactly: the transpiled circuit is simulated as a density matrix, the
outcome distribution of each basis is read without sampling, the device's readout channel is
applied analytically, and the estimators of \cref{dfn:counts} are evaluated on that distribution
(\py{docsgen.physics.mitigation_exact}). The same computation with the full noise model
reproduces the sampled means of Table~\ref{tab:m2} to within their spread (e.g.\ HVA, $\theta=0$:
\VMxHvaZeroFullRaw, \VMxHvaZeroFullRo, \VMxHvaZeroFullSym, \VMxHvaZeroFullRoSym).

\begin{prop}[Filtering then correcting over-corrects]\label{prop:overcorrect}
  Assume independent readout errors with $e_0^{(q)},e_1^{(q)}\le e$, no other noise, and a
  noise-free distribution supported on $|b|=n/2$. Then the filtered distribution is
  $p+O(e^2)$, and to leading order the filter-then-correct estimator returns
  \[
    E_{\mathrm{ro+sym}}=\sum_t c_t\,\langle P_t\rangle\prod_{q\in S_t}\frac{1}{1-e_0^{(q)}-e_1^{(q)}}
      \;+\;O\bigl(e^2,\ \max_q|e_0^{(q)}-e_1^{(q)}|\bigr),
  \]
  a multiplicative over-correction of each two-body term by about $1/(1-2e)^2$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  A single bit flip changes $|b|$ by one, so every read string of weight $n/2$ arises either from
  no flip, with probability $p(b)\prod_q(1-e^{(q)}_{b_q})$, or from at least two flips, with
  probability $O(e^2)$. The kept fraction is $\prod_q(1-e_{b_q}^{(q)})+O(e^2)$, and dividing
  gives $\tilde p=p+O(e^2)$. The filtered shots are therefore already almost free of readout
  error, but the estimator still applies $v_q$. Writing
  $v_q(b)=s(b)/(1-e_0^{(q)}-e_1^{(q)})+(e_0^{(q)}-e_1^{(q)})/(1-e_0^{(q)}-e_1^{(q)})$ and taking
  the product over a two-site support gives
  $\prod_{q\in S_t}s(b_q)\big/\prod_{q\in S_t}(1-e_0^{(q)}-e_1^{(q)})$ up to the stated
  corrections. Averaging over $\tilde p$ gives the formula.
\end{proof}

With the calibrated $e=\VMtReadoutErr$, the predicted factor $1/(1-2e)^2$ is \VMxOverPredicted.
The exact computation with readout noise only gives $E_{\mathrm{ro+sym}}/E_{\mathrm{sym}}=\VMxOverFactor$:
readout + symmetry returns \VMxHvaZeroRoRoSym{} (\VMxHvaZeroRoRoSymRel) at the exact HVA
parameters, where readout correction alone is exact (\VMxHvaZeroRoRo).

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Readout noise alone} gives readout + symmetry an overshoot of
    \VMxHvaZeroRoRoSymRel{} (HVA, $\theta=0$) and \VMxRvbPtRoRoSymRel{} (RVB,
    $\theta=\VBoundTheta$), as \cref{prop:overcorrect} predicts.
  \item \textbf{Gate noise alone} leaves a bias that symmetry filtering cannot
    remove. The sector-aware estimator, which treats readout consistently, isolates it:
    \VMxHvaZeroFullCtfRel{} and \VMxRvbPtFullCtfRel.
  \item \textbf{Under full noise}, to leading order readout + symmetry equals the symmetry-only
    energy times the over-correction factor: $\VMxOverFactor\times\VMxHvaZeroFullSym=\VMxPredictFull$,

    over-correction (\VMxOverPct) happen to be nearly equal on this device.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{rem}[Consequence]
  The small bias of the chosen stack is a property of \py{FakeGuadalupeV2}'s ratio of readout
  error to gate error, not of the method. On a device with relatively less gate noise, or after
  depth compression (M4) removes CX gates, the same stack will move further below the
  noise-free value. Readings below $-18$ in an optimisation are therefore expected, not
  accidental. The sector-aware estimator is principled but leaves \VMxHvaZeroFullCtfRel, and it works with
  quasi-probabilities, so its variance is larger; it was computed here only in the
  infinite-shot limit.
\end{rem}

Figure~\ref{fig:m2b} and Table~\ref{tab:m2b} show the following.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Readout + symmetry is now honest.} Readout noise only:
    \VMbHvaRoPtRoSymRel{} (HVA) and \VMbRvbRoPtRoSymRel{} (RVB), as \cref{prop:ctf} predicts.
    Gate noise only: \VMbHvaGatesPtRoSymRel{} and \VMbRvbGatesPtRoSymRel. Full noise:
    \VMbHvaFullZeroRoSymRel{} (HVA, $\theta=0$), which the infinite-shot computation predicted
    before the rerun (\VMxHvaZeroFullCtfRel, Table~\ref{tab:m2exact}).
  \item \textbf{Adding twin rescaling removes most of the gate residual}:
    \VMbRoSymTwinMin{} to \VMbRoSymTwinMax{} wherever there is gate noise, always positive, and
    \VMbHvaRoPtRoSymTwinRel{} (HVA) and \VMbRvbRoPtRoSymTwinRel{} (RVB) with readout noise alone,
    zero within error. It works on each source separately, so it is not a cancellation.
    \Cref{sec:m2twin} explains why it works and why a small positive bias remains.
  \item \textbf{Readout + ZNE is the fallback} for ansatzes without a symmetry:
    \VMbRoZneMin{}--\VMbRoZneMax{} wherever there is gate noise, at \VMbShotsZne{} shots per
    energy.
  \item \textbf{Symmetry and ZNE must not be combined}: \VMbRoSymZneMin{} to
    \VMbRoSymZneMax{} wherever there is gate noise, the same on gate noise alone as on full noise.
  \item \textbf{The twin needs the filter.} Without it, HVA's twin overshoots on gate noise
    alone (\VMbHvaGatesPtTwinRel); with it, HVA's twin works.
\end{itemize}
Under gate noise the filter keeps \VMbKeptMinSym{}--\VMbKeptMaxSym{} of the raw shots; after
correction the kept quasi-weight is \VMbKeptMinRoSym{}--\VMbKeptMaxRoSym. With readout noise only it
is \VMbHvaRoPtRoSymKept, as \cref{prop:ctf} requires.

\subsection{Why the filter rescues the twin}
\label{sec:m2twin}

By \cref{prop:twinerr}, rescaling is exact only if target and twin are damped alike. The
development session's hypothesis is that the residual noise after filtering damps singlet states
almost uniformly. This report did not prove it; the exact infinite-shot computation of
\cref{sec:m2cancel}, extended to gate noise alone and to the twin parameters, measures how close
to uniform the damping is.

\cref{prop:twinerr} the revised stack converges to a small positive bias, not to zero:
\VMxTwMin{} to \VMxTwMax{} under full or gate noise (Table~\ref{tab:m2twinbias}). Under readout
noise alone it is exact, since \cref{prop:ctf} makes both $D$ equal to $1$.

\paragraph{Sampled against the limit.} The first reruns (\py{m2b}) reused one calibration and
one twin measurement for all repeats, so their spread left out the twin's uncertainty: for HVA
at $\theta=0$ it was \VMoHvaFullZeroRoSymTwinSpread{} against a reported standard error of
\VMoHvaFullZeroRoSymTwinSe{} per energy, and the bias looked like at most \VMoMaxRoSymTwin. With
a fresh set-up per repeat (\texttt{2116da4}) the two agree (\VMbHvaFullZeroRoSymTwinSpread{}
and \VMbHvaFullZeroRoSymTwinSe; Table~\ref{tab:m2berr}), and the sampled biases rise to
\VMbRoSymTwinMin{}--\VMbRoSymTwinMax. Seven of the eight settings lie within two standard errors
of their infinite-shot limit (Table~\ref{tab:m2twinbias}). The exception, HVA with full noise at
$\theta=0$, has $z=\VMbZHvaFullZero$.

To locate it, undo the rescaling repeat by repeat. The twin energies are recorded per set-up,
and the target energy each repeat measured is
\[
  E_{\mathrm{noisy}}(\theta)=E_{\mathrm{mit}}\,\frac{E_{\mathrm{noisy}}(\theta_{\mathrm t})}{E_{\mathrm{ideal}}(\theta_{\mathrm t})} .
\] Table~\ref{tab:m2twinsplit} compares both means
with their exact values. For HVA with full noise the target agrees ($z=\VMbTargetZHvaFullZero$)
and the twin does not ($z=\VMbTwinZHvaFullZero$). To first order the excess bias is the sum of
the two shifts, $\VMbTwinShiftHvaFullZero+\VMbTargetShiftHvaFullZero$, on top of the limit
\VMxTwHvaZeroFullCtfRel. Both full-noise HVA rows used the same eight twin draws (the recorded
draws are identical in the two runs), so this is one fluctuation counted twice. The same twin
with gate noise alone agrees ($z=\VMbTwinZHvaGatesPt$). The other component outlier, the RVB
target at $\theta=0$ ($z=\VMbTargetZRvbFullZero$), pulls its row the other way.

How surprising is this? With \VMbRepeats{} repeats the standard error is itself uncertain by about
\VMbSpreadRelErr, so $z$ follows a Student distribution with $\VMbRepeats-1$ degrees of freedom.
If every setting sat exactly at its limit, a row with $|z|\ge\VMbZHvaFullZero$ among the eight
would occur with probability \VMbPRow, and \VMbCompOver{} or more of the \VMbCompN{} independent
components (the shared twin counted once) with $|z|>\VMbCompCut$ with probability \VMbPComp. The deviations are therefore suggestive, not
conclusive. A systematic part was possible: the limit uses the device's exact readout channel,
while each repeat calibrates with \VMbSetupRo{} shots, which also pick up gate errors in the
calibration circuits. A systematic would persist with new seeds; a fluctuation would not.

\paragraph{Check with new seeds.} At this report's suggestion the development session reran the
outlying setting (HVA, readout + symmetry + twin, $\theta=0$) with seeds \VMqSeedA{} and
\VMqSeedB, \VMqRepeats{} fresh-set-up repeats each, under full noise and under gate noise alone
(\py{evaluate_fixed} directly; the numbers are quoted from \py{docs/roadmap.md}, not regenerated
from \py{results/}). Table~\ref{tab:m2seeds} applies the same split. Under full noise the twin
returns to its exact energy ($z=\VMqTwinZFullA$ and $\VMqTwinZFullB$), and the rows sit at

fluctuation in the twin, and any full-noise systematic from the finite calibration is below the
precision of these runs, about \VMqRowSemFullA.

\subsection{Problems solved and open}

\begin{itemize}
  \item[\checkmark] Four mitigation layers, composable, on Aer and the IBM sampler; unit-tested.
    Exact standard errors per shot, a bootstrap for \cref{dfn:ctf}.
  \item[\checkmark] Fixed-point evaluation with a below-ground flag and a noise-source switch:
    every stack's bias and cost measured with full, readout-only and gate-only noise.
  \item[\checkmark] The first chosen stack's accuracy was found to be a cancellation
    (\cref{sec:m2cancel}) and withdrawn. The revised stack is accurate on each error source
    separately: sampled bias \VMbRoSymTwinMin{}--\VMbRoSymTwinMax{} with gate noise, infinite-shot
    limit \VMxTwMin{}--\VMxTwMax, zero with readout noise alone.
  \item[\checkmark] Calibration and twin are re-measured per repeat (\texttt{2116da4}), so the
    reported spreads include the set-up noise.
  \item[\checkmark] Mitigated optimisations reach parameters within \VMwIdealErrHvaSpsa{} (HVA)
    and \VMwIdealErrRvbPs{} (RVB) of the ground energy (noise-free), and the estimates read
    \VMwFinalHvaSpsa{} and \VMwFinalRvbPs{} instead of about \VPostFinalRvbAdam.
  \item[$\circ$] \textbf{The twin's damping is not exactly the target's}: the remaining bias of
    up to \VMxTwMax{} comes from a \VMxDampSpreadHvaCtf{} damping mismatch (HVA), which the
    hypothesis of near-uniform damping does not explain.
  \item[$\circ$] \textbf{Symmetry and ZNE must not be stacked} as implemented.
  \item[$\circ$] \textbf{Both new pieces stop at about 20 qubits}: correct-then-filter inverts
    the readout channel on all $2^n$ outcomes, and the automatic twin needs a statevector. M3
    lattices need replacements (for example, inverting only on the sampled strings, and Clifford
    or matrix-product-state twins).
  \item[$\circ$] \textbf{Hardware checks H0 and H1 are skipped}, so neither the noise floor nor
    the mitigation has been tested against a real device.
  \item[$\circ$] \textbf{The instance is easy} (\cref{rem:easy}): at $\theta=0$ the state is a
    singlet product, which symmetry filtering suits unusually well. The generic point confirms
    the trend, but M3 instances are needed before generalising.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Exercises}

\begin{exer}
  A qubit has $e_0=0.02$ and $e_1=0.03$. Compute $v(0)$ and $v(1)$, and check
  $\mathbb E[v(\tilde b)\mid b=1]=-1$.
\end{exer}
\begin{sol}
  $1-e_0-e_1=0.95$, so $v(0)=(1-0.03+0.02)/0.95=0.99/0.95\approx1.0421$ and
  $v(1)=-(1+0.03-0.02)/0.95=-1.01/0.95\approx-1.0632$. Given $b=1$, the read bit is $0$ with
  probability $e_1=0.03$, so the mean is $0.03\cdot1.0421+0.97\cdot(-1.0632)=0.03126-1.03126=-1.0000$.
\end{sol}

\begin{exer}
  Under readout noise only, why is symmetry filtering by itself \emph{not} exact
  (Table~\ref{tab:m2exact}: \VMxHvaZeroRoSymRel), although it removes every single-flip error?
\end{exer}
\begin{sol}
  Two flips in opposite directions ($0\to1$ on one qubit and $1\to0$ on another) keep the weight
  at $n/2$ and pass the filter. They occur with probability $O(e^2)$ per shot, but there are
  many qubit pairs: about $\binom{12}{2}\,e^2\approx66\times(0.022)^2\approx3\,\%$ of shots carry
  a double flip, a fraction of which pass. A pair flip inside a bond changes that bond's $Z_iZ_j$
  reading only if exactly one of its qubits flips, which biases the energy upward by a fraction
  of a percent.
\end{sol}

\begin{exer}
  Use \cref{prop:twinerr} and Table~\ref{tab:m2twin} to predict the infinite-shot bias of twin
  rescaling for HVA at $\theta=0$ under gate noise, with and without the filter. Which one
  overshoots, and why?
\end{exer}
\begin{sol}
  Without the filter, $1-D/D_{\mathrm t}=1-\VMxDampHvaZeroGtRaw/\VMxDampHvaTwinGtRaw=\VMxTwHvaZeroGtRawRel$:
  the twin is damped more than the target, so the factor is too large and the estimate
  overshoots below the noise-free value. With the filter,
  $1-\VMxDampHvaZeroGtCtf/\VMxDampHvaTwinGtCtf=\VMxTwHvaZeroGtCtfRel$: the twin is now damped
  slightly less, and a small part of the bias remains. (Computed from unrounded $D$.)
\end{sol}

\begin{exer}
  Why does correct-then-filter give exactly the same energy under full noise as under gate
  noise alone (Table~\ref{tab:m2exact} and \cref{sec:m2twin}), while filter-then-correct does
  not?
\end{exer}
\begin{sol}
  By \cref{prop:ctf}, inverting the exact readout channel recovers the pre-readout
  distribution $q$, which is the same with and without readout noise; everything after that
  depends only on $q$. Filter-then-correct applies the filter to $Aq$, whose sector part is not
  $A$ applied to the sector part of $q$: single flips leave the sector, so the filtered
  distribution is already almost readout-free, and the subsequent correction over-corrects
  (\cref{prop:overcorrect}).
\end{sol}
